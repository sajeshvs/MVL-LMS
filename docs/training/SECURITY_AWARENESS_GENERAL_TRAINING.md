## Purpose

This training ensures that all personnel understand MVL Group's security expectations, recognize their own role in protecting company, client, and controlled information, and know how to prevent and report potential information-security incidents.

## Positioning Statement

MVL Group operates in an environment with strict contractual, client, and regulatory information-protection requirements. We maintain a strong security posture aligned with recognized cybersecurity practices, including CMMC Level 2 requirements where applicable, inside our Microsoft GCC High environment. Every employee — not just IT or the security team — has a responsibility to protect information and use company systems securely.

## Core Messages

- Security is everyone's responsibility, regardless of job title, project, location, or whether you work directly with government-related contracts.
- MVL Group handles several categories of information: public, internal, confidential, client/customer, personal, financial, project, and controlled information.
- Some information carries additional U.S. Government safeguarding requirements, including Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI).
- Not everyone is authorized to access or handle CUI. Employees who are will receive separate, detailed, role-based training.
- If you receive, find, or suspect restricted information you weren't expecting or aren't authorized to access, **stop, avoid sharing it, and report it immediately.**
- Sensitive or controlled information must only be used in company-approved systems, locations, and communication channels.
- Company, client, project, personal, and controlled information must **never** be uploaded to personal storage, personal email, unapproved file-sharing apps, public websites, or unapproved AI tools.

## Module 1: Our Security Commitment

Our security posture protects our people, our projects, our clients, our partners, our systems, and our reputation. It also helps us meet contractual and regulatory requirements that keep MVL Group eligible for the work we do.

Security is not only an IT responsibility. Every person who accesses MVL Group information or systems has a role in protecting it — from the field to the front office.

This commitment supports five objectives:

- Protect business operations and project delivery.
- Safeguard client and employee information.
- Meet contractual, legal, and cybersecurity obligations, including CMMC Level 2.
- Reduce the risk of cyberattacks, data loss, fraud, and accidental disclosure.
- Maintain MVL Group's eligibility for sensitive customer and government-related work.

## Module 2: Information We Protect

MVL Group handles many kinds of information every day. Understanding the broad categories — and what's expected of you for each — is more useful day-to-day than memorizing regulatory terms.

| Information type | Examples | Your responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Company information** | Strategy, financial data, internal reports, policies, pricing | Use only approved company systems; share only with those who need it |
| **Client and project information** | Drawings, specifications, project controls, contracts, estimates, procurement data | Follow project access rules and approved collaboration channels |
| **Personal information** | Employee, customer, supplier, payroll, ID, and contact information | Share only when necessary and authorized |
| **Controlled information** | Government-related technical or contract information with specific handling requirements | Access and handle only if authorized and trained; report unexpected receipt immediately |

In certain circumstances, controlled information may include U.S. Government **Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)**, which carries specific protection requirements under our contracts and under NIST SP 800-171. You don't need to memorize the regulation — you just need to recognize that "controlled" means extra care, and that access requires authorization.

## Module 3: Secure Ways of Working

These are the everyday habits that apply to everyone at MVL Group, no matter your role:

- Use strong, unique passwords, and always use multi-factor authentication (MFA) on your company account.
- Keep devices locked whenever you step away — even for a minute.
- Use only approved company tools and storage locations (our SharePoint, OneDrive for Business, and other systems inside our GCC High tenant) — never personal cloud storage.
- Confirm recipients before sending files, links, payment information, or sensitive emails.
- Be cautious of unexpected links, attachments, login prompts, invoices, and requests for urgent action — these are classic phishing and fraud tactics.
- Never reuse your MVL Group password for personal accounts.
- Never share accounts, passwords, access badges, or MFA codes with anyone — including coworkers and IT.
- Report lost devices, suspected phishing, misdirected emails, unusual system behavior, or suspected data exposure promptly — don't wait to be sure.
- Use only company-approved AI tools (for example, Microsoft 365 Copilot within our GCC tenant). **Never** enter sensitive, client, project, personal, confidential, or controlled information into public or unapproved AI tools, even to save time.

## Module 4: Restricted and Controlled Information

Some MVL Group and client information has additional access and protection requirements. This may include controlled information tied to specific client, contractual, or government-related work — potentially including CUI.

You may see labels, handling instructions, restricted project locations, or access limitations on this information. If information is marked or appears restricted, only access or share it if you have a clear business need and have been authorized to do so.

Keep these rules in mind:

- Do not assume access is permitted just because you work for MVL Group or are on the same project.
- Do not forward, download, print, copy, photograph, screenshot, or upload restricted information unless you are authorized.
- Do not use personal email, WhatsApp, personal drives, or any other unapproved collaboration tool to move this information.
- Do not enter controlled or suspected-controlled information into public AI tools.
- **If you are unsure, pause** and contact IT Security (security@mvl-group.com), the project information owner, Contracts, or your manager.

Employees who are authorized to work with CUI and other controlled information will receive separate, detailed, role-based training beyond this course — this module is awareness, not a handling procedure.

## Module 5: What to Do If Something Feels Wrong

Use this simple action model whenever something seems off — an unexpected file, a suspicious email, a document that looks like it shouldn't have reached you:

### Pause → Protect → Report

**Pause**
Do not click, forward, download, upload, print, or share.

**Protect**
Leave the information in its current, approved location. Do not delete or alter it unless IT Security instructs you to.

**Report**
Use one of MVL Group's approved reporting channels right away:

- **Report phishing or a security incident:** security@mvl-group.com, or post directly in the Teams channel **#🚨-incidents** — this reaches the SOC and the on-call CISO immediately.
- **Raise a routine IT/Security ticket:** the MVL Service Desk portal at **https://itsm.mvl-group.com**, or the Teams channel **#🎫-helpdesk-queue**. Target response times are under 4 hours for urgent (P1) issues and under 24 hours for standard (P2) requests.
- **Urgent, active incident (data actively being exposed or shared):** Post in Teams **#🚨-incidents** first, then follow up by email to security@mvl-group.com. This is the same path used for MVL Group's broader incident escalation procedure.
- **Suspected misdirected controlled-information or CUI email:** Do not forward it or reply-all. Email security@mvl-group.com and compliance@mvl-group.com immediately so the message can be contained, recalled where possible, and logged correctly.

## Closing Statement

You do not need to be a cybersecurity expert to protect MVL Group. Your role is to work securely, use approved tools, protect information according to its sensitivity, and report anything suspicious or unexpected without delay.

Employees who are authorized to work with controlled information, including CUI where applicable, will receive additional role-based training.
